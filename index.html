Mirror stack

A deliberate wedge: shim one side of the back film so the films are a fraction of a degree apart and the tunnel bends towards the narrow side. Each copy moves sideways about z² × angle / (2 × gap); 0.5 to 1° shows clearly, which is a 5 to 10 mm difference in gap across 60 cm.

Live show

A full audio-visual show in four scenes, in the style of a festival AV set: monolithic colour fields with a strobing square, concentric rings with tracked type, particle bursts, and a 3D plexus network that shatters on the hits. It listens for itself: kick, snare and hats, the tempo, the bars, breakdowns, builds and drops.

Scene

Its own ears: it reads the raw sound before Audio gain and ignores Follow the melody. Every detector is levelled against its own recent peak, so quiet and loud tracks hit the same. Kick, snare and hats are sudden rises in their own frequency band (sub below 110 Hz, snare body 150–400 Hz plus crack 1.5–5 kHz, hats above 7 kHz); the tempo comes from the time between kicks; a breakdown is the sub falling away, a drop is the sub slamming back on a kick, and the bar count restarts on it. In Auto it changes scene on the phrase, picking what fits: hard kicks for the colour fields, filter sweeps for the rings, busy hats and builds for the particles, pads and broken beats for the plexus.

Flash safety: never more than 3 white flashes in any second and no red flashes, the photosensitive-epilepsy limit (WCAG 2.3.1, Ofcom). Kicks faster than that cut to cyan instead. Put a warning sign up anyway when the strobes are on.

PUDE logo show

The PUDE cube with the hand out of the hole, and almost never the logo itself: the cube tumbles, explodes, melts, unfolds, slides its letters round like a Rubik's cube and drops letters out, while the hand never stops: it waves, pumps, fist-bumps the crowd, points at you, lures you closer, counts down to the drop and much more. Same ears as the live show above: kick, snare, hats and melody, the beat clock, bars and phrases, breakdowns, builds and drops.

The hand
The cube

How it follows the music. Every move is locked to the beat clock: waves reach their turn on the beat, pumps and stomps land on it, the fist bump hits the crowd on the first beat of the bar. Kicks squash the cube and send rings out down the tunnel, snares slide the letter rows, lift the top half, fire the finger guns and snap the fingers, hats make the fingers tremble and the letters pop, the melody makes the hand talk and spins the star in the hole, a filter sweep spins the hand. In Auto the hand changes every few bars on the downbeat and the cube on the phrase, picking what fits: four-on-the-floor gets fist pumps and rave hands, busy hats get finger drums, a melody gets the talking hand, a breakdown gets "come here", hiding and peeking and a melting or ghost cube. In a build the hand counts the last 5 beats down to the phrase where the drop should land; on the drop it bursts into jazz hands and the cube explodes. Black stays black, so the tunnel copies stay clean; the hand is drawn in the logo's own pink most of the time.

    Sources

    Demo beat drives the audio visuals without a microphone. Pick Video sound to drive them from the video that is playing.

    The visuals follow the melody: the note of the loudest instrument or synth (110 Hz to 1.4 kHz, so the bass and kick stay out). The note sets the colour, round the circle of fifths so related keys get related colours; how high or low it is drives each infinity-tunnel pattern in its own way, and every new note can send a ring.

    DJ position

    The podium is a 60 cm deep plank on hinges along the back of the trailer, with two fold-out legs. Its lowest setting is wheel-centre height; up to the top of the mudguard the wheel sticks up through it, so it needs a half-round notch to fold up, smaller the higher it sits. The decks are two Pioneer CDJ-3000 (11.8 cm high) and a DJM-900NXS2. Good height: Digital DJ Tips gives 102 cm for men and 91 cm for women, DJ tables run 83 to 106 cm, and DJs put the controls halfway between hip and elbow, about 0.575 × body height above their feet.

    Booth items

    Snap each item to a spot along the desk and to the DJ's edge or the back, against the tops. A DJ set of two players sits either side of the mixer when you snap it to the same spot and row. The decks (mixer and DJ set) stand on the riser from DJ position; the laptop and VJ panel stay on the desk. The DJ stands in front of the decks.

    Smoke machine

    A 1200 W fog machine (Antari Z-1200 class, 2.5 L tank) on a steel shelf against the right sub, nozzle towards the dancefloor.

    Glycol fog droplets of about a micron scatter light forwards and hardly absorb it, so beams show best when you look towards the light. A puff leaves the nozzle at a few m/s, slows down, spreads and turns into haze that fills the hall; ventilation and slow evaporation clear it. The heater can't keep up with non-stop output: when it cools, the machine pauses until it is hot again. Figures are typical for this class of machine, not one model's data sheet.
