Mirror stack

A deliberate wedge: shim one side of the back film so the films are a fraction of a degree apart and the tunnel bends towards the narrow side. Each copy moves sideways about z² × angle / (2 × gap); 0.5 to 1° shows clearly, which is a 5 to 10 mm difference in gap across 60 cm.

    Smoke machine

    A 1200 W fog machine (Antari Z-1200 class, 2.5 L tank) on a steel shelf against the right sub, nozzle towards the dancefloor.

    Glycol fog droplets of about a micron scatter light forwards and hardly absorb it, so beams show best when you look towards the light. A puff leaves the nozzle at a few m/s, slows down, spreads and turns into haze that fills the hall; ventilation and slow evaporation clear it. The heater can't keep up with non-stop output: when it cools, the machine pauses until it is hot again. Figures are typical for this class of machine, not one model's data sheet.

    DJ position

    The podium is a 60 cm deep plank on hinges along the back of the trailer, with two fold-out legs. Its lowest setting is wheel-centre height; up to the top of the mudguard the wheel sticks up through it, so it needs a half-round notch to fold up, smaller the higher it sits. The decks are two Pioneer CDJ-3000 (11.8 cm high) and a DJM-900NXS2. Good height: Digital DJ Tips gives 102 cm for men and 91 cm for women, DJ tables run 83 to 106 cm, and DJs put the controls halfway between hip and elbow, about 0.575 × body height above their feet.

    Finds the tempo of the song (60 to 200 BPM) from its rhythm and locks the Infinity tunnel patterns to it: rings go out on every beat, the chase laps once a bar (twice or four times when the bass gets heavy), polygons, stars and the spiral move with a push on each beat, and the whole tunnel runs faster for a fast song. The beat keeps going through a break. Glitches follow the song: a snare tears a few rows sideways, a drum fill makes the motion stutter, a drop makes the tunnel jump one mirror copy with a colour shift, a big leap in the melody shifts the colours, and every 8 bars the picture twitches. Glitches move or recolour the light but never flash it, so they stay inside the 3 flashes a second limit. Only the Infinity tunnel patterns follow it.

    Hit sensitivity: how far above its recent average a kick, snare or hat has to jump to count as a hit. Lower = more hits. Used by the live show, the tunnel show and the PUDE shows. (The Director and VJ clips still use their own copy of this value.)

    How a drop is found: first a breakdown, the sub bass falling away for a while after it has been steady; then the drop is a kick whose sub jumps back clearly more than the high crack band (a snare roll or noise riser lifts both), or the sub plainly back for good. The bar count restarts on it, so everything locked to bars lands with the drop. More sensitive: a shorter breakdown is enough and a weaker sub return counts. The same ears drive the live show in Text show, and drop sensitivity reaches the tunnel show and the Director too.

    One flash gate for every white flash: the live show, the tunnel show, the PUDE shows and the Director's white screens. At least 0.15 s between two flashes, and never more than this many in any second. 3 a second is the photosensitive-epilepsy limit (WCAG 2.3.1); a flash that the gate refuses is shown as a cut or dim instead. The Director also keeps its own 3-a-second timer on top, and VJ clips have their own 3-a-second strobe.

      Body

      The screen as a window through the trailer: it shows the part of the DJ that stands right behind it, at real size, in a silly costume. A 1 m screen shows 1 m of DJ. What part you see comes from the DJ position tab: the height of the DJ, on the ground or on the podium (and how high), the decks and the riser. The DJ dances along on the live show's ears.

      Costume

      How it moves: the knees bend on every beat and the hips sway, one heel lifts on every other beat, the head nods. Kicks push the dip deeper, hats rattle the skeleton's bones and blink the astronaut's lights, a snare clacks the skull's jaw and pops a coin for the 8-bit hero. In a breakdown the dancing calms down; in a build one hand goes up and pumps on the beat; on the drop the DJ jumps (floating, for the astronaut). In Auto the costume changes on the phrase and on every drop.

      Bear metamorphosis

      The orange bear from the drawing sheet, flowing for ever through its 12 stages, from the cute teddy to the screaming monster and back. It does not cross-fade: the eyes narrow, the brows come down and the mouth tears open along motion measured between every two drawings. The music decides how monstrous it gets and how fast it changes, and bends and swirls it. It keeps its own orange, yellow and red; only at the peaks of the music does it go psychedelic. Black in the drawing stays black: those LEDs are off, so the mirror copies stay clean.

      Layout
      Psychedelic colours

      How monstrous. The loudness against the song's own average, builds and drops pull the bear towards the monster; breakdowns and quiet parts bring the teddy back. It never stands still: it keeps morphing back and forth through a few stages round that point, faster with busy hats and a dense kick, and over a few minutes it wanders through all 12 anyway. Every kick snarls it a little further for a moment.

      Its own colours, psychedelic at the peaks. Most of the time the bear has the drawing's orange fur, yellow muzzle and ears and red eyes. The psychedelic colours come in during a build, for the bars after a drop and in the loudest moments, flash up briefly on a chord from outside the key, and then fade back. The red eyes always stay red. When it does go psychedelic, the harmony picks the colours, from its own ears for notes: the peaks of the fine spectrum folded onto the 12 notes (a chromagram), the chord from that (24 major and minor chords, the bass note helps) and the key from the last seconds (Krumhansl profiles). The chord colours the fur and its third the muzzle, so major and minor look different. Colours go round the circle of fifths, measured from the key: chords in the key stay warm neighbours, a chord from outside the key jumps to a far colour.

      The sound moves it. The sub bends the picture slowly like liquid, the mids swirl it round the middle, the hats send fine ripples through it, noisy or distorted sound makes it boil, pure tones keep it smooth. The beat breathes it, kicks punch it closer, the melody tilts it up and down, a snare flashes the colours, the brightness of the sound adds colour bands to the fur. In Auto the layout changes on the phrase, picking what fits: the single bear when it is calm, the kaleidoscope with synths, the grid with busy hats, the vortex in builds. On a drop it roars: it rushes in from far away as the monster.

      Hand and Cube

      The PUDE cube with the hand out of the hole, and almost never the logo itself: the cube tumbles, explodes, melts, unfolds, slides its letters round like a Rubik's cube and drops letters out, while the hand never stops: it waves, pumps, fist-bumps the crowd, points at you, lures you closer, counts down to the drop and much more. Same ears as the live show in Text show: kick, snare, hats and melody, the beat clock, bars and phrases, breakdowns, builds and drops.

      How it follows the music

      How it follows the music. Every move is locked to the beat clock: waves reach their turn on the beat, pumps and stomps land on it, the fist bump hits the crowd on the first beat of the bar. Kicks squash the cube and send rings out down the tunnel, snares slide the letter rows, lift the top half, fire the finger guns and snap the fingers, hats make the fingers tremble and the letters pop, the melody makes the hand talk and spins the star in the hole, a filter sweep spins the hand. In Auto the hand changes every few bars on the downbeat and the cube on the phrase, picking what fits: four-on-the-floor gets fist pumps and rave hands, busy hats get finger drums, a melody gets the talking hand, a breakdown gets "come here", hiding and peeking and a melting or ghost cube. In a build the hand counts the last 5 beats down to the phrase where the drop should land; on the drop it bursts into jazz hands and the cube explodes. Black stays black, so the tunnel copies stay clean; the hand is drawn in the logo's own pink most of the time.

      Flash safety: the white flash of the hit goes through the same limit as the live show (at most 3 flashes a second, White strobes in Advanced visuals). The broken screen flickers by rows and blocks, not by the whole panel, and whole-panel dips go through the same limit.

      Live show

      A full audio-visual show in four scenes, in the style of a festival AV set: monolithic colour fields with a strobing square, concentric rings with tracked type, particle bursts, and a 3D plexus network that shatters on the hits. It listens for itself: kick, snare and hats, the tempo, the bars, breakdowns, builds and drops.

      Scene

      Its own ears: it reads the raw sound before Audio gain and ignores Follow the melody. Every detector is levelled against its own recent peak, so quiet and loud tracks hit the same. Kick, snare and hats are sudden rises in their own frequency band (sub below 110 Hz, snare body 150–400 Hz plus crack 1.5–5 kHz, hats above 7 kHz); the tempo comes from the time between kicks; a breakdown is the sub falling away, a drop is the sub slamming back on a kick, and the bar count restarts on it. In Auto it changes scene on the phrase, picking what fits: hard kicks for the colour fields, filter sweeps for the rings, busy hats and builds for the particles, pads and broken beats for the plexus.

      Flash safety: never more than 3 white flashes in any second and no red flashes, the photosensitive-epilepsy limit (WCAG 2.3.1, Ofcom). Kicks faster than that cut to cyan instead. Put a warning sign up anyway when the strobes are on.

      Tunnel show

      Made for the infinity mirror: black tunnels, square or round, an edge chase round the frame and beat rings flying into the tunnel, all as thin lines and single sparks on black, so the mirror stacks every copy into one deep tunnel instead of a grey haze. It has the live show's ears and reads five sides of the sound: which frequency band plays, how each hit fades, how clean or distorted it sounds, how much space there is (reverb and stereo), and where the song is going.

      Tunnel shape

      Frequency. The sub bass only moves the whole picture, never details: on a kick the black mouth of the tunnel contracts and the frame flashes, so every mirror copy lights at once. The mids bend and stretch the tunnel walls: the rings wobble with the synths and spread outwards when a filter opens. The hats and air only make single sparks and tiny twinkles on the walls.

      Envelope. Every hit lights up on the very frame it is heard, with no smoothing, and then fades exactly as fast as that sound fades: the decay time of kick, snare, hats and melody is measured from the song itself after every hit.

      Timbre. How noisy the spectrum is between 260 Hz and 5 kHz, against the song's own clean level, tells distortion from pure tones. Overdrive tears rows sideways, makes blocks and splits the colours (red and blue copies of the tunnel); a pure piano or sine gives thin, pale, perfectly clean lines and nothing else.

      Space. A long tail after the hits (reverb) pulls the camera back: a deeper tunnel with more rings, a smaller mouth and a faint haze; dry sound keeps it shallow and razor sharp. Stereo turns the tunnel towards the side the sound pans to, a wide stereo image twists it, and the edge chase leans to that side.

      The song. A build adds chaos step by step: the tunnel twists, the walls shudder, more comets chase round faster (a lap a bar, then every 2 beats, then every beat), more sparks, rings on every eighth note. A breakdown takes 90 % of the motion away, leaving the black mouth breathing on the sub. The last beat before an expected drop goes black; the drop blows the tunnel open with a white flash, a shock ring and eight comets.

      Flash safety: white flashes share the live show's limit, at most 3 in any second, the frame flashes included. Glitches move or recolour the light, they never flash it.

      Palette

      Each colour is the most saturated version this LED type can make in that direction, solved from its own red, green and blue. Hover a chip for its CIE x, y.

      The visuals follow the melody: the note of the loudest instrument or synth (110 Hz to 1.4 kHz, so the bass and kick stay out). The note sets the colour, round the circle of fifths so related keys get related colours; how high or low it is drives each infinity-tunnel pattern in its own way, and every new note can send a ring.
